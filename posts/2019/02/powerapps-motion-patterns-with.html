UPDATE (August 2026)

Just recently, while modernizing this blog and dragging 18 years of content onto a new look, I landed back on this very post and realized two things: the parametric equations are every bit as beautiful as they were in 2019, and the download link above, sadly, no longer works, as the old PowerApps Community Apps Gallery has since been retired and the link now drops you on a generic community homepage. That just wouldn't do!

So, I rebuilt the whole experiment from scratch as a single canvas app called Parametric Motions, and this time around the app does quite a bit more than the original: each curve gets its own screen, a small circle traces the pattern across a dark plotting surface leaving a colored trail behind it, and a set of sliders lets you change the equation parameters live (frequencies and phase for the Lissajous, petal count for the Rose, revolutions for the Spiral, and the classic R, r, and d trio for the Hypotrochoid). Move a slider and the trace restarts with the new shape. Very cool! And if you are curious how far it has come, you can always visit the original video of the 2019 version, which is also embedded just above.

You may be asking how the trail gets drawn, since canvas apps still have no native plotting surface for this sort of thing. Very simple: a repeating Timer control advances a step counter, and an Image control renders an inline SVG polyline whose points come from a Concat(Sequence(...)) formula evaluating the equations at 600 samples along the curve. Everything in the app derives from that one counter plus the slider values, so pause, resume, and restart are each a one line formula, and that's always a good thing.

As it turns out, the tooling has changed quite a bit since 2019 as well. Canvas apps can now be authored as readable YAML source code (the *.pa.yaml format), so instead of a binary msapp attachment sitting in a long gone gallery, the full source now lives on GitHub together with a Dataverse solution wrapper and a build script that packs and imports the whole thing with the Power Platform CLI:

  • github.com/dgpblogster/power-apps-fun (the app lives in the parametric-motions folder, and the README walks you through cloning, authenticating, and deploying)

NOTE: After importing the solution, be sure to open the app for edit in Power Apps Studio once, then save and publish. Apps packed from YAML source are validated on first open.

Suffice to say, I did not type every line of YAML myself: the rebuild was pair-built with Claude, Anthropic's AI coding agent, working against Microsoft's power-platform-skills guidance and the pac CLI, from the parametric formulas all the way down to the solution import. The math has not changed since 1815; the tooling most certainly has!
