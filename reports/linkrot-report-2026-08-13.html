Read this first: what the numbers mean

An 18-year archive pointing heavily at retired Microsoft properties was always going to look like this. 88% of posts carry at least one dead link, but the deadness is highly concentrated: PartnerSource/CustomerSource (retired), blogs.msdn.com (retired), SkyDrive, old KB URLs, and defunct event sites account for most of it. A dead PartnerSource link in a 2009 payroll-tax post is historically inert — it harms no reader and no SEO worth chasing. The actionable subset is much smaller and is called out below.

Honest caveats: the ~930 "suspicious" links are NOT confirmed dead. 429/999 responses are rate-limiting from scanning 12-wide (LinkedIn and this blog's own blogspot self-links dominate that bucket — the self-links redirect fine in a browser). Many 403s are bot-blocking, not deadness. blogs.msdn.com URLs (442 of the suspicious) mostly redirect to Microsoft's Learn archive — degraded, not always gone. Treat "suspicious" as "needs a human click if the post matters."
